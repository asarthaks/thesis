\begin{center}
    {\Large\bfseries Abstract}
\end{center}

{\renewcommand{\baselinestretch}{1.12}\normalsize
\noindent Controllable text generation asks a language model to produce text satisfying a global property that cannot be checked until a sequence is complete. One prominent family pursues this without retraining: it treats the frozen likelihood of a pretrained autoregressive language model as an \emph{energy function} over sequences and samples from it with gradient-guided Langevin dynamics, adding an arbitrary constraint at inference time. This thesis tests the premise that construction rests on, that the gradient of the frozen likelihood is a usable local search direction on discrete text.

\noindent The study covers five energy functions across 145 configurations on masked-token recovery on WikiText-2 validation, with a token-space and an embedding-space sampler. The central result is negative and consistent: the input-embedding gradient provided no reliable proposal advantage over a norm-matched random direction, exact recovery was 0.0\% in 139 of the 145 configurations, and a $5 \times 5$ sweep over step size and proposal temperature, spanning proposal entropies from uniform to deterministic, finds no configuration at which the gradient helps.

\noindent Two measurements locate the failure. First, a \emph{linearization failure}: the first-order surrogate by which the discrete sampler ranks candidates is uncorrelated with the true change in sequence energy ($|\rho| < 0.06$ on all five energies). Second, and decisively, the deficiency belongs to \emph{which} derivative is taken. The one-hot input gradient, which is what the discrete samplers this family adapts actually use, correlates at $\rho = 0.60$ to $0.73$ at admissible distances, and in the same sampler recovers 40\% of tokens on GPT-2 and 41\% on Llama-3, where the input-embedding gradient recovers at most 2\%. The usable direction exists in closed form on the model's output side; taking the input-embedding derivative is what discards it.

\noindent Amortizing the search with a GFlowNet did not escape the failure: sampling on the tuned energy was indistinguishable from sampling on the untuned base. A ladder of proposals inside one exact-energy chain locates what actually matters. A uniform draw recovers 0.5\%, matching the gradient-guided sampler; the model's own left-to-right conditional recovers 23.5\%; a score-entropy diffusion model 39\%; and a bidirectional masked language model, which is \emph{not} score-trained, 44.5\%. The operative variable is what a proposal may condition on, not the objective it was trained with. The practical implication is direct: on a frozen autoregressive model, search the energy with output-side quantities rather than differentiating it with respect to its input embeddings.
