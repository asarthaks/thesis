\section{Conclusion}
\label{ch:conclusion}

This thesis set out to build controllable text generation on faithful Langevin dynamics, treating the frozen likelihood of an autoregressive language model as an energy function and steering it at inference time with an additive constraint. It tested the premise that construction rests on, and found the premise did not hold in the settings examined.

\paragraph*{What was tested.} One hundred and forty-five sampling configurations across five energy functions, a supervised fine-tuned GPT-2 Large, three GFlowNet-tuned variants of it, and a Llama-3 8B cross-architecture control, on a masked-token recovery benchmark and on a free-form continuation task. Two samplers were run, one remaining in token space and one operating in the embedding space, under a factorial combination of Metropolis--Hastings correction, gradient normalization, and annealing schedule length, alongside five diagnostic experiments and a diffusion positive control at two scales.

\paragraph*{What was found.} In the tested token-substitution settings, following the input-embedding gradient of the frozen autoregressive likelihood was no more effective than following a norm-matched random direction, and exact recovery of the corrupted token was $0.0$ percent in $139$ of the $145$ configurations, never exceeding one percent in the remaining six. The mechanism is that the first-order surrogate the discrete sampler ranks by is uncorrelated with the true change in energy across the whole admissible range of substitution distances, and that the input-embedding gradient is structurally blind to how well a candidate token fits its own left context, exactly so at the final position under the shifted causal indexing. That blindness is a consequence of which derivative is taken, not of the training objective: the same measurements show that the one-hot gradient, which the discrete samplers this literature adapts actually use, carries the discarded term in closed form and correlates with the true energy change at $\rho = 0.60$ to $0.73$ over the admissible range. Substituted into this thesis's own sampler, on the same frozen models and the same corrupted sequences, it recovers forty percent of tokens on GPT-2 Large and forty-one percent on Llama-3, against at most two percent for the input-embedding gradient anywhere in a sweep spanning proposal entropies from uniform to deterministic. The signal the samplers needed was in the frozen model throughout, and the choice of derivative is what discarded it. Two further findings qualify the null itself, and they pull in opposite directions. The discrete proposal is numerically uniform over the vocabulary at every step of every configuration in the main grid, so that grid could not have detected an informative gradient had one been present; a sweep over step size and proposal temperature that spans the full range from a uniform to a deterministic proposal repairs that gap and finds no configuration at which the gradient outperforms a norm-matched random direction. Conversely, the cases in which the gradient appeared reliably \emph{worse} than a random direction did not survive scrutiny: they were produced by an asymmetry in how the Metropolis--Hastings correction was applied across the compared arms, and they disappear once the reverse-proposal term is computed exactly for every arm. The energy itself is not at fault: a single top-$k$ rescoring pass and a gradient-free Gibbs sampler both recover coherent text on the identical energy, at a fraction of the cost. Applied faithfully, the Metropolis--Hastings correction improves the discrete sampler and disables the continuous one, the acceptance ratio collapsing through its proposal term at the cell boundaries a nearest-neighbour projection creates. On these models the lowest-energy text is also the most degenerate, so the objective the methods pursue is not the objective one would want. Amortizing the search with a GFlowNet did not escape the failure in the tested setup: three collapse modes appeared and Langevin sampling on the tuned energy was indistinguishable from sampling on the untuned base, although the tuning had moved that energy by tens of nats. As a positive control, a score-trained diffusion model on the same tokenizer supplied a clearly positive local direction where the autoregressive gradient was statistically zero and, substituted as the proposal inside this thesis's own exact-energy chain, raised exact recovery from zero to thirty-nine percent. A ladder of proposals run in that same chain then localizes the cause more sharply than the objective: a uniform draw over the vocabulary recovers half a percent, which is what the gradient-guided sampler achieves; the frozen model's own left-to-right conditional recovers twenty-three and a half percent; and a bidirectional masked language model that was never trained with a score objective recovers forty-four and a half percent, more than either diffusion model tested. What separates a working proposal from a failing one is what it is allowed to condition on, not how it was trained.

\paragraph*{What remains uncertain.} The combined GFlowNet and diffusion results locate the failure outside the sampling algorithm, and the masked-language-model control narrows it further to what a proposal can condition on rather than to the training objective as such, but that remains an interpretation rather than a proof covering all autoregressive language models: the GFlowNet result bounds the variants trained here, not amortized inference in general, and the diffusion pilots use smaller, differently trained models on a qualitative comparison. On the frozen autoregressive landscape, plug-and-play constraint steering failed; on a score-trained diffusion landscape it became partly possible, subject to fluency and classifier-alignment limits, and how far that extends beyond one noisy guide at this scale is not settled here.

\paragraph*{The main practical implication.} The experiments refute the premise that the frozen autoregressive sequence-likelihood gradient can supply the required local score for this class of Langevin-based methods without additional training. They do not refute the training-free premise as such, since gradient-free training-free methods remained viable throughout, in this study's own baselines as well as in the published work of Section~\ref{sec:rel-energy}. For a practitioner building inference-time control on a frozen autoregressive model, the usable object is the energy and its output-side conditionals, not its input-embedding gradient. For the gradient-guided route to be recovered, the model would have to be trained to supply the score the route assumes, which is what the diffusion control indicates and what a score-matching fine-tune of the sequence density would attempt directly.
